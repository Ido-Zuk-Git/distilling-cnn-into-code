# AGENTS.md -- binding rules for this workspace

## Task

Write `classify.py` in this directory. It must define `predict(path: str) -> str` that returns one of the 10 Imagenette class names: tench, english_springer, cassette_player, chain_saw, church, french_horn, garbage_truck, gas_pump, golf_ball, parachute. It is scored on 2,000 unseen images of these classes.

**Target: 68.90%.** That is the hidden-test accuracy of a small CNN trained from scratch on exactly the 505 training images you have. You are given that CNN (see 'The teacher CNN'). **Your goal is to distill the CNN into explainable strategies formulated as code, without any statistical learning**, and reach its accuracy that way.

## The one hard rule: no statistical learning

Nothing in `classify.py` may be derived from data by an automated procedure. Concretely:

- No fitting of any kind: no gradient descent, least squares, regression, clustering across images, PCA, nearest-neighbour or exemplar tables, no averages/histograms/statistics computed over training images and stored in the program, no automated search over thresholds or kernel values whose result is written into the program.
- No pretrained anything: no model or weight files, no OpenCV `dnn`, `ml`, `face` or cascade classifiers, no default people/face detectors, nothing that loads parameters from a file or a library.
- No embedded data: no base64/zlib/hex blobs, no lookup keyed on filename or hash, no numeric array pasted from any computation over images.
- No numbers from the CNN: you may not copy, round, or re-express any weight, bias, statistic or activation value from the teacher into `classify.py`. Describe what a filter or channel does, then write your own by formula with constants you chose and explained.
- No fitting to the teacher: nothing in `classify.py` may be fitted to the teacher's outputs or activations, by script or by hand-search over values.

## The teacher CNN

`teacher/` holds a small CNN trained from scratch on exactly your 505 training images. It scores 68.90% on the hidden test set. Its architecture is summarised in `teacher/README.md`. Using it is mandatory, and it comes first:

How it was trained (`teacher/README.md` has the details): 5 blocks of conv3x3-BN-ReLU-maxpool (32-64-128-256-256 channels), global average pool, linear 256->10; input 128 px (resize shortest side to 144, crop 128); AdamW (lr 2e-3, weight decay 0.05) with a one-cycle schedule, label smoothing 0.1, batch size 64, 40 epochs (8 steps per epoch, 320 steps in all); augmentation: random crop from the 144 px resize, horizontal flip, brightness/contrast/saturation jitter; final-epoch weights, no early stopping or model selection. It reaches 99.0% on its training images, 68.1% on the validation images and 68.90% on the hidden test.

- **Inspect the teacher before you inspect any image.** <!-- IF LOOK -->`look.py` refuses to work until the teacher has been used. <!-- ENDIF -->You may read its weights, and you may run it on training images and read its activations. You may use any mechanistic interpretability method you want to understand the weights or the activations. What to look for in them is up to you.
- **Let the teacher's operation guide your strategy.** Decide what your program computes, and how it combines it, from what you learn about how the CNN works, and keep consulting the teacher as you iterate, not only once at the start. In NOTES.md, record what you examined in the teacher and what you wrote because of it.
- **Everything you write must still stand on its own.** The comments must explain each step in terms of the images (what it computes, which visual property of which classes it captures), and every constant must be one you chose. The hard rule applies in full: no numbers from the CNN, no fitting to the teacher. Distillation here means understanding what the CNN does and re-expressing it as strategies you can explain, not reproducing its numbers.

## Program constraints

- One self-contained file, any size.
- **Fast and vectorised.** Compute with numpy, scipy and cv2 operations over whole arrays; no Python loops over pixels, and no separate full-image pass per hypothesis when one pass can serve several. The scorer's per-image timeout is 1 s and a timed-out image counts as wrong; aim for a mean well under 100 ms per image (`check.py` and `step.py` print it). Imports: `numpy`, `PIL`, `scipy`, `cv2` (OpenCV, hand-designed functionality only: filters, morphology, contours, Hough, feature descriptors such as SIFT/ORB, colour spaces; the modules and functions listed above are banned and the scorer rejects them), and the standard library.
- `predict` must not read any file other than the image passed in (open it with PIL; `cv2.imread` and other cv2 file readers are rejected by the scorer), and must not use the network, subprocesses, `eval`/`exec`, or environment variables. Enforced: the scorer copies `classify.py` alone into an empty directory and imports it there; per-image timeout 1 s.
- **Every non-trivial line must be explained by a comment**: what it computes, and why (which visual property of which classes it captures). Every numeric constant must be explained, either by its own comment or by a comment that covers the group it belongs to (for example one comment above a block of score weights saying what they balance and how you chose them). Trivial lines (imports, plain assignments, returns, loop headers) need none. A reader must be able to understand the whole classifier from the comments alone. An uncommented non-trivial line is a rule violation, not a style issue, and `check.py` and `step.py` refuse to run a file that has numeric lines without a nearby comment.
- Your rules must generalise. The score is on images you have never seen. A rule that fits a training photo but would not hold for a typical photo of that class is worthless.
- Do not download anything, do not read files outside this workspace, do not look for the test set.

## You are the orchestrator

How you use the training and validation sets, in what order, in what granularity and with what loop, is your decision: nobody prescribes a strategy. You may work image by image, in batches, in epochs, class by class, or in any other way. What is fixed: the teacher comes first; the training images are yours to compute on<!-- IF LOOK --> and view<!-- ENDIF -->; the validation set reports only its overall accuracy (`check.py` on `data/val` prints the accuracy and nothing else); the hard rule.

You may delegate. `python3 agent.py "task"` starts a fresh sub-agent (same model, same rules, same workspace, no memory of your conversation) that works on the task you give it and returns its final report; it can read and write files in this workspace. Give it any job: writing or refactoring code, inspecting the teacher, auditing `classify.py` against the rules, analysing a set of failures, trying an alternative strategy in a separate file. Run several at once with `&` and `wait`. Sub-agents share your wall-clock and budget, cannot be viewed by you while they run, and know only what you put in the task text and what they read from the workspace, so write tasks the way you would brief a colleague: goal, files to read, what to deliver, what is forbidden. You remain responsible for the deliverables and for everything a sub-agent does under the rules. Only you delegate: a sub-agent must not run `agent.py` itself.

Write your strategy down in NOTES.md before you start and whenever you change it, with the reason. Record every measurement (train and val) with what version of `classify.py` it refers to.

## How to work

- Diagnose first. Before changing the program, find out how it fails on training images: which classes, confused with what, on what kind of photo. Then find out what the teacher does on those images.
- Localise causally. That a channel of the teacher is more active on one class is not enough: show that the property it responds to separates the classes in your own measurements before you build a rule on it, and show that a new rule changes the outcome on the images it was written for without breaking others.
- Measure with enough power. Accuracy over n images has a standard error of about 50/sqrt(n) percentage points: 2.2 points on the 495 val images, 7 points on 50 images of one class. Do not read a difference smaller than that as a result.
- Plan your val passes. They are the only estimate of the hidden-test score, and you have {{VAL_BUDGET}}. Spend them on decisions, not on every edit; use the training set and the teacher for everything else.
- Work efficiently. State the hypothesis of every analysis script in its first comment line; load things once; print compact summaries, not whole arrays.

## How the session runs

The session continues until all {{VAL_BUDGET}} val passes are used; finishing early with passes left is not an end, the harness restarts you with a reminder. To keep the conversation short, the harness may restart it at any point ("handoff"): you then get the task again, the tail of your NOTES.md, and whatever is on disk, and nothing else. Write NOTES.md so that a fresh start can continue from it: strategy, every measurement with the version it refers to, what is in progress, what was tried and failed.

## Working practice

- Keep `classify.py` runnable at all times; the file on disk is what gets scored.
- Keep `NOTES.md` updated with your strategy, every train and val measurement, what you delegated and what came back, and what you tried, including what did not work.
- Do not get stuck in a local minimum. If several iterations of the same strategy each gain little, that is a signal to step back and try a genuinely different overall strategy, measured against the current one, rather than adding more rules to the old one.
- Stop only when the val passes are used up or the wall-clock limit ends the session.
